# Interview Validity Research: Meta-Analytic Findings

## Overview

This document synthesizes findings from major meta-analyses on employment interview validity, spanning over 100 years of personnel psychology research. The evidence consistently shows that **structured interviews are approximately twice as predictive of job performance** compared to unstructured interviews.

## Key Meta-Analyses

### Schmidt & Hunter (1998, 2016)
**"The Validity and Utility of Selection Methods in Personnel Psychology"**

The landmark study that analyzed 85+ years of research on 19 different selection methods. This work established the empirical foundation for modern evidence-based hiring.

**Sample Size:** Meta-analysis of hundreds of studies representing millions of job candidates

**Key Finding:** Structured interviews combined with cognitive ability testing provide the highest predictive validity of any practical selection combination.

### McDaniel, Whetzel, Schmidt, & Maurer (1994)
**"The Validity of Employment Interviews: A Comprehensive Review and Meta-Analysis"**

Analyzed 245 validity coefficients from 86,311 individuals across diverse job types and organizational settings.

**Key Findings:**
- Interview validity varies substantially based on content, structure, and criterion type
- Structured interviews consistently outperform unstructured interviews
- Situational and behavioral interviews show particularly high validity

## Validity Coefficients Explained

### What Is a Validity Coefficient?

A validity coefficient (r) measures the correlation between a selection method and job performance:

- **r = 0.0:** No relationship (random chance)
- **r = 0.1-0.2:** Weak relationship
- **r = 0.3-0.4:** Moderate relationship
- **r = 0.5-0.6:** Strong relationship (rare in personnel selection)
- **r = 0.7+:** Very strong relationship (extremely rare)

**Note:** Even "moderate" validity coefficients (0.3-0.4) can have substantial practical impact when making thousands of hiring decisions. A validity of 0.5 is considered excellent for personnel selection.

### Interpreting Practical Impact

A validity coefficient of 0.5 means:
- **25% of variance in job performance** is explained by interview scores (r² = 0.25)
- Candidates scoring in the **top third** on interviews perform on average at the **63rd percentile** in job performance
- Organizations using this method will make substantially better hiring decisions than those using less valid methods

## Structured vs. Unstructured Interviews

### The Core Comparison

| Aspect | Structured Interview | Unstructured Interview |
|--------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Validity Coefficient** | **0.51** | **0.38** |
| **Variance Explained** | 26% | 14% |
| **Relative Improvement** | **+34% better prediction** | Baseline |
| **Bias Reduction** | Up to 50% less adverse impact | Higher bias |
| **Interviewer Consistency** | High (with training) | Low |
| **Legal Defensibility** | Strong | Weak |
| **Candidate Perception** | Fairer | More "personal" |

### Why the Difference?

**Structured interviews are more valid because they:**

1. **Reduce noise:** Same questions for all candidates eliminates variability from different question difficulty
2. **Enable comparison:** Can't compare candidates fairly if they answered different questions
3. **Focus on job:** Predetermined questions ensure job-relevance
4. **Reduce bias:** Less room for subjective preferences to influence evaluation
5. **Capture evidence:** Better documentation enables review and calibration

**Unstructured interviews are less valid because they:**

1. **High variability:** Each interview is different, making comparison difficult
2. **Confirmation bias:** Interviewers ask questions that confirm their initial impression
3. **Halo effect:** One strong/weak answer colors perception of all subsequent answers
4. **Similar-to-me bias:** Interviewers favor candidates who remind them of themselves
5. **Recency bias:** Recent information weighted more heavily than earlier responses

## Effect of Interview Structure Components

Research shows that multiple components contribute to validity:

### Decomposing "Structure"

| Component | Validity Impact |
|-----------|----------------|
| Standardized questions (same for all candidates) | ++ |
| Predetermined follow-up questions | + |
| Behavioral/situational question content | +++ |
| Multiple interviewers | ++ |
| Scoring rubrics | +++ |
| Interviewer training | ++ |
| Note-taking requirements | + |
| Panel evaluation (vs. individual) | ++ |

**Key Finding:** The more structure components you implement, the higher the validity. No single component is sufficient - they work synergistically.

### Question Type Matters

Not all structured interviews are equal - content matters:

| Question Type | Validity (r) | Example |
|--------------|-------------|---------|
| **Behavioral** (past behavior) | **0.55** | "Tell me about a time when..." |
| **Situational** (hypothetical) | **0.50** | "What would you do if..." |
| Job knowledge | 0.48 | "How would you debug X?" |
| Background/credentials | 0.38 | "Where did you work before?" |
| Self-assessment | 0.10 | "Rate yourself 1-10 on..." |
| Hypothetical self-description | 0.15 | "What kind of leader are you?" |

**Best Practice:** Mix behavioral and situational questions, avoid self-assessment.

## Combining Selection Methods

### Multivariate Validity

Using multiple selection methods in combination can increase validity beyond any single method:

| Combination | Multivariate Validity | Incremental Validity |
|-------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **GMA + Integrity Test** | **0.65** | Best combination |
| **GMA + Structured Interview** | **0.63** | Highly practical |
| **GMA + Work Sample** | **0.63** | Expensive but effective |
| GMA + Conscientiousness | 0.60 | Self-report limitation |
| GMA + Unstructured Interview | 0.55 | Less than structured |

**Key Insight:** Structured interviews add substantial incremental validity beyond cognitive ability testing alone. The combination is more powerful than either method in isolation.

### Why Combinations Work

1. **Different constructs:** GMA measures learning ability; interviews measure applied skills and motivation
2. **Complementary:** Cognitive ability shows "can they learn?"; interviews show "will they apply it?"
3. **Non-redundant:** Low correlation between methods means each adds unique information

## Adverse Impact and Fairness

### Adverse Impact Ratios

Adverse impact occurs when a selection method has substantially different pass rates for protected groups. The 4/5ths rule (80% rule) is a common threshold.

| Selection Method | Typical Adverse Impact |
|-----------------|----------------------|
| Cognitive Ability Tests | High (0.50-0.70) |
| **Structured Interviews** | **Low-Moderate (0.75-0.90)** |
| Unstructured Interviews | Moderate-High (0.60-0.80) |
| Work Samples | Low (0.80-0.95) |
| Personality Tests | Very Low (0.90-1.00) |

**Note:** Lower numbers = more adverse impact. Structured interviews show 25-50% less adverse impact than unstructured.

### Why Structured Interviews Reduce Bias

**Mechanisms:**
1. **Standardization:** Same questions eliminate differential difficulty
2. **Documentation:** Written records enable bias detection
3. **Rubrics:** Explicit criteria reduce subjective judgment
4. **Training:** Makes interviewers aware of bias patterns
5. **Panel review:** Multiple perspectives catch individual bias

**Research Finding:**
> "Structured interviews decreased differences between demographic groups by up to 50% compared to unstructured approaches." - Google re:Work data

## Moderators of Interview Validity

### What Affects How Well Interviews Predict Performance?

**Factors that INCREASE validity:**
- More structure (standardized questions, rubrics, training)
- Behavioral and situational content
- Multiple trained interviewers
- Panel evaluation vs. individual
- Job analysis basis for questions
- Note-taking requirements
- Separation of evidence collection from decision

**Factors that DECREASE validity:**
- Unstructured, conversational format
- Questions about background/credentials only
- Single untrained interviewer
- Immediate hire/reject decisions
- No job analysis
- No documentation requirements
- Interviewer also makes hiring decision

### Job Type Moderators

| Job Type | Interview Validity | Notes |
|----------|-------------------|-------|
| Managerial/Professional | 0.54 | Higher validity |
| Technical/Skilled | 0.52 | Good validity |
| Sales | 0.50 | Moderate validity |
| Clerical | 0.48 | Slightly lower |

**Explanation:** Interviews may be slightly more predictive for complex jobs where judgment and interpersonal skills are critical. However, validity remains substantial across all job types.

## Predictive Validity vs. Face Validity

### The Perception Gap

**Interviewer Perceptions:**
- Most interviewers rate their own interviewing as "above average"
- Unstructured interviews *feel* more informative
- Personal conversation feels like it reveals "true character"
- Gut feelings seem accurate in the moment

**Actual Data:**
- Unstructured interviews have only moderate validity (r=0.38)
- Structured interviews are 34% more predictive
- Gut feelings are heavily influenced by bias
- Most interviewers cannot accurately assess their own accuracy

**Quote from research:**
> "Interviewers everywhere think they're good at interviewing, but the data rarely supports this confidence."

### Why the Disconnect?

**Psychological Factors:**
1. **Confirmation bias:** We remember hits, forget misses
2. **Overconfidence:** Natural tendency to overestimate expertise
3. **Illusory correlation:** We see patterns that don't exist
4. **Hindsight bias:** Success seems "obvious" in retrospect

**The Solution:**
Measure actual outcomes. Track interview scores vs. job performance to calibrate accuracy assessment.

## Temporal Validity

### How Long Does Predictive Validity Last?

**Research Finding:**
Interview validity remains relatively stable over time:

| Time Since Hire | Validity Coefficient |
|----------------|---------------------|
| 3 months | 0.52 |
| 1 year | 0.51 |
| 3 years | 0.49 |
| 5+ years | 0.45 |

**Interpretation:**
Structured interviews predict both immediate and long-term job performance. Slight decline over time likely due to:
- Job changes over tenure
- Additional training and development
- Environmental factors accumulating

## Practical Implications

### Return on Investment

**Scenario:** Company hires 100 people per year using unstructured interviews (r=0.38)

**If they switch to structured interviews (r=0.51):**
- **34% improvement** in prediction accuracy
- Estimated **$2-3M annual value** from better hiring decisions (conservative estimate based on salary and productivity)
- **Reduced turnover** from better job fit
- **Lower legal risk** from more defensible process

**Time Investment:**
- Upfront: ~40 hours to develop questions and rubrics for one role
- Per interview: ~40 minutes saved from pre-made materials (Google data)
- **Breakeven:** After ~5-8 interviews, time-neutral; then saves time

### Validity ≠ Perfection

**Important Context:**
- Even 0.51 validity means interviews aren't perfect (only 26% of variance explained)
- Other factors affect job performance: training, management, team dynamics, org changes
- Interviews should be **one component** of a comprehensive hiring system
- Combine with other valid methods (cognitive ability, work samples, etc.)

**Realistic Expectations:**
Structured interviews will:
- ✓ Substantially improve hiring decisions
- ✓ Reduce (but not eliminate) bias
- ✓ Provide defensible, consistent process
- ✗ NOT guarantee perfect hires every time
- ✗ NOT eliminate all subjectivity
- ✗ NOT work without proper implementation

## Implementation Recommendations Based on Research

### Minimum Viable Structure

To achieve meaningful validity improvements, implement at minimum:

1. **Same questions for all candidates** in same role
2. **Behavioral or situational question content** (not just background)
3. **4-level scoring rubric** with behavioral anchors
4. **Basic interviewer training** (2-3 hours)
5. **Written notes** documenting candidate responses

**Expected Validity:** ~0.45 (substantial improvement over unstructured)

### Optimal Structure

For maximum validity approaching 0.51+:

1. ✓ Standardized questions with predetermined follow-ups
2. ✓ Mix of behavioral and situational questions based on job analysis
3. ✓ Detailed behaviorally anchored rating scales (BARS)
4. ✓ Comprehensive interviewer training and calibration (8+ hours)
5. ✓ Multiple trained interviewers per candidate
6. ✓ Detailed note-taking requirements
7. ✓ Panel review separating interviewing from decision-making
8. ✓ Regular validation studies (scores vs. performance)

**Expected Validity:** ~0.51-0.55

## Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1: "Unstructured interviews let me assess culture fit better"

**Reality:**
- "Culture fit" often means "similar to me" (similarity bias)
- Unstructured assessment of fit has near-zero predictive validity
- If culture fit is job-relevant, define it behaviorally and assess it structurally

### Myth 2: "I can tell in the first 5 minutes if someone will be good"

**Reality:**
- First impressions are heavily influenced by appearance, confidence, and similarity bias
- Research shows these snap judgments are no better than chance
- Actual predictive information emerges from structured questioning

### Myth 3: "Structured interviews feel too rigid and scripted"

**Reality:**
- Candidates rate structured interviews as MORE fair and satisfying
- Training helps interviewers deliver questions naturally
- Follow-up questions can still be conversational
- Structure is in evaluation, not in rapport-building

### Myth 4: "We hire for unique roles, so we can't use standard questions"

**Reality:**
- Questions can be role-specific and still structured
- Standardization means "same for all candidates FOR THIS ROLE"
- Even unique roles have definable competencies
- Every role benefits from structured assessment

### Myth 5: "The best candidates will go elsewhere if we make them do structured interviews"

**Reality:**
- Top candidates prefer fair, transparent processes
- Research shows 35% higher candidate satisfaction with structure
- Unstructured interviews actually deter quality candidates who've been burned by bias
- Competition for talent requires legitimate assessment

## Future Research Directions

Active areas of ongoing research:

### Video/Remote Interviewing
- Does medium (in-person vs. video) affect validity?
- Early data suggests comparable validity with proper structure
- May reduce bias by focusing attention on content vs. appearance

### AI and Machine Learning

- Risk: Automating existing biases if not carefully implemented

### Asynchronous Interviews
- Recorded responses vs. real-time: validity comparison
- Equity implications of asynchronous format
- Standardization benefits vs. rapport challenges

### Cross-Cultural Validity
- Do validity coefficients hold across cultures?
- Cultural differences in communication styles
- Need for culture-specific behavioral anchors

## Key Takeaways

1. **Structured interviews (r=0.51) are ~34% more predictive than unstructured (r=0.38)** - This is a massive practical difference.

2. **Combination with cognitive ability testing (r=0.63) is optimal** - Use multiple validated methods for best results.

3. **More structure = higher validity** - Every structural component adds incremental validity.

4. **Behavioral and situational questions outperform credential-based questions** - Ask about what people did/would do, not what they have.

5. **Structured interviews reduce bias by 25-50%** - Fairer AND more accurate.

6. **Interviewer confidence ≠ interviewer accuracy** - Measure outcomes, don't trust gut feelings.

7. **Implementation requires investment, but ROI is substantial** - Time breaks even quickly, then saves time and money.

8. **Validity holds across job types and over time** - Generalizable finding, not context-specific.
