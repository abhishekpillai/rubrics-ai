# Karat: Equity-Focused Technical Interviewing

Source: Karat Research and Case Studies (2024)

## Overview

Karat is the world's leading technical interviewing platform, conducting hundreds of thousands of interviews annually for companies like Atlassian, Roblox, and others. Their extensive data set has revealed evidence-based practices for creating more equitable interview processes while maintaining quality and efficiency.

## Core Equity Principles

Karat's approach is built on the recognition that traditional technical interviews systematically disadvantage underrepresented groups, not because these candidates lack skills, but because the process itself introduces bias at multiple points.

### The Equity Challenge in Technical Hiring

**The Problem:**
- URM and female candidates typically have fewer interview opportunities
- Interview anxiety disproportionately affects underrepresented groups
- Subjective evaluation amplifies unconscious bias
- Traditional processes favor candidates who "look like" existing team members

**Karat's Solution:**
- Redo interviews for second chances
- Standardized questions and rubrics
- Resume-free initial screening
- Trained interviewer network
- Separation of evaluation from hiring decision

## Breakthrough Innovation: Redo Interviews

### What Are Redo Interviews?

Karat offers every candidate the opportunity to redo their technical interview if they're not satisfied with their performance, for any reason. No questions asked.

### The Data (2024)

**Adoption by Demographic:**
- URM candidates are **30% more likely** to take a redo interview than other candidates
- Black candidates particularly benefit from this option

**Performance Improvements:**
- **Nearly 60% of Black candidates** improve their scores in the redo
- **41% increase** in close rate for female candidates who use redo option
- Overall hiring yield increased by **17%** for companies offering redos

**Business Impact:**
- **Over 1,000 candidates hired** after completing a redo interview
- These hires perform comparably to those who passed on first attempt
- Companies would have missed these qualified candidates without redo option

### Why Redo Interviews Work

**Addresses Performance Anxiety:**
First interviews often involve:
- Unfamiliarity with format
- Technical setup issues
- Nervousness that impairs performance
- Misunderstanding of expectations

Second interviews typically show:
- Better technical setup (learned from first)
- Reduced anxiety (familiar format)
- Clearer understanding of expectations
- Ability to demonstrate true capability

**Levels the Playing Field:**
- Candidates from majority groups often have more interview practice
- Networking privileges provide "insider knowledge" of formats
- Multiple opportunities are standard for well-connected candidates
- Redo formalizes second chances that informally exist for some

**Quality Signal:**
- Taking a redo shows persistence and learning ability
- Improvement demonstrates growth mindset
- Both are job-relevant competencies

### Implementation: Atlassian Case Study (Oct 2024)

**Context:**
Atlassian wanted to improve diversity in technical hiring while maintaining high quality bar and candidate experience.

**Implementation:**
- Offered redo interviews through Karat platform
- Tracked outcomes by demographic group
- Monitored quality of hires from redo pathway

**Results:**
- "Candidates who self-identified as an underrepresented minority and completed a Redo, in particular, go on to complete all the interview stages and successfully receive an offer"
- Karat provided data on underrepresented racial and ethnic groups (URGs) performance and scheduling patterns
- Data allowed Atlassian to identify opportunities to better support these groups
- No compromise in quality - redo hires performed comparably

**Key Insight:**
The redo interview revealed that many candidates failed first interviews due to factors unrelated to their actual technical ability.

## Standardized Interview Process

### Resume-Free Initial Screening

**Traditional Process Problem:**
- Names signal race/ethnicity/gender
- University names create pedigree bias
- Employment gaps penalize caregivers
- Credential inflation favors privileged backgrounds

**Karat's Resume-Free Approach:**
- Candidates assessed purely on technical interview performance
- No resume review until after technical bar is met
- Hiring teams see interview performance first
- Reduces bias before interview even happens

**Data Impact:**
Creates more equitable opportunities by evaluating candidates on job-relevant skills rather than proxies.

### Standardized Question Bank

**The Approach:**
- Every candidate for a given role receives questions from the same standardized bank
- Questions are job-relevant and tested for validity
- Level of difficulty is consistent across all candidates
- Prevents advantage from "insider knowledge"

**Benefits:**
- **Transparency:** Candidates know they're assessed on same criteria
- **Equity:** No advantage from networking or previous exposure
- **Legal defensibility:** Consistent standards across all candidates
- **Predictive validity:** Focus on job-relevant skills

### Consistent Evaluation Rubrics

**The System:**
- Scoring rubrics establish core competencies and rating system
- All interviewers use the same definitions of performance levels
- Rubrics focus on observable behaviors, not subjective impressions
- Scores are separated from hiring recommendations

**Why It Matters:**
- Minimizes bias in evaluation
- Generates stronger, more accurate hiring signal
- Enables comparison across interviewers
- Allows review for bias detection

**Quote from Karat:**
"Scoring rubrics help ensure that candidates are evaluated consistently by establishing core competencies and a rating system that all interviewers use, ultimately minimizing bias and generating a stronger, more accurate hiring signal."

## Quality Control and Bias Detection

### Separation of Scoring from Recommendation

**The Practice:**
Interviewers provide:
1. Scores based on rubric
2. Detailed notes on what candidate did/said
3. **No hiring recommendation**

Hiring teams:
1. Review scores and notes from all interviewers
2. Look for patterns across evaluations
3. Make hiring decision based on aggregate data
4. Can identify individual interviewer bias

**Why This Matters:**
- Prevents single interviewer bias from dominating
- Allows others to review feedback objectively
- Makes aberrations visible that may indicate bias
- Creates accountability through peer review

### Interview Review Process

**Karat's Quality Assurance:**
"The entire interview should be reviewed by a fellow interviewer to ensure that aberrations in the interview process haven't compromised its accuracy."

**What Gets Reviewed:**
- Adherence to standardized questions
- Quality and objectivity of notes
- Appropriateness of follow-up questions
- Scoring consistency with rubric
- Any signs of bias or unprofessionalism

**Benefits:**
- Maintains interviewer quality
- Detects bias patterns
- Ensures process consistency
- Provides interviewer feedback for improvement

## Three Core Best Practices

From Karat's research on making interview processes more effective:

### 1. Use Standard Questions for Every Candidate

**The Practice:**
Create a list of interview questions and ask every candidate the same questions.

**Why:**
- **Equitable:** Puts all candidates on a level playing field
- **Transparent:** Candidates understand assessment criteria
- **Enjoyable:** Candidates appreciate fairness
- **Consistent:** Enables meaningful comparison

**Data:**
Standard questions are the foundation of an equitable, transparent, and enjoyable candidate experience.

### 2. Implement Structured Scoring Rubrics

**The Practice:**
Establish core competencies and rating system that all interviewers use.

**Why:**
- **Consistent evaluation:** All interviewers use same standards
- **Minimizes bias:** Reduces subjective judgment
- **Stronger signal:** More accurate prediction of job performance
- **Accountability:** Makes aberrations visible

**Implementation:**
Define what poor/mixed/good/excellent looks like for each competency using behavioral indicators.

### 3. Separate Scoring from Recommendations

**The Practice:**
Interviewers score and document evidence; hiring committees make decisions.

**Why:**
- **Enables review:** Others can check for bias
- **Identifies aberrations:** Patterns of bias become visible
- **Reduces influence:** Single interviewer can't dominate decision
- **Improves accuracy:** Aggregate data more reliable than individual opinion

## Equity-Focused Interview Conduct

### The "Golden Rules" of Technical Interviews

Based on Karat's extensive experience conducting technical interviews:

**1. Focus on Problem-Solving Process, Not Just Solution**
- How candidates think matters more than memorized answers
- Multiple valid approaches exist for most problems
- Collaboration and communication are job-relevant skills

**2. Provide Consistent Support Across All Candidates**
- Clarify questions when asked
- Provide hints using standardized approach
- Don't let candidates struggle unnecessarily
- Equal help maintains fairness

**3. Evaluate Observable Behaviors, Not Impressions**
- Document what candidate said/did, not what you felt
- Avoid interpretation ("seemed nervous" → "paused before answering")
- Focus on job-relevant competencies
- Check assumptions against rubric

**4. Ignore Non-Signals**
- Communication style (unless job-relevant)
- Accent or language differences
- Nervousness or interview anxiety
- Personality traits unrelated to job

## Addressing Common Objections

### "Won't redo interviews lower the quality bar?"

**Data Says No:**
- 1,000+ candidates hired after redo perform comparably to first-pass hires
- Taking a redo demonstrates persistence and learning (job-relevant traits)
- Quality bar is the same - candidates must still meet technical standard
- First interview failures often due to anxiety, technical issues, or unfamiliarity with format - not lack of skill

### "Isn't resume-free screening impractical?"

**Implementation:**
- Use resume to filter for basic qualifications (e.g., can legally work, has technical background)
- Conduct technical interview without resume details
- Only after passing technical bar do hiring teams see full background
- Separates "can do the job" from "looks impressive on paper"

### "How do we justify extra cost of redo interviews?"

**ROI Analysis:**
- 17% increase in hiring yield = more hires from same candidate pool
- 1,000+ hires that wouldn't have happened = expanded talent pool
- Improved diversity = better team performance (research-backed)
- Reduced bias = legal risk mitigation
- Better candidate experience = stronger employer brand

### "What if candidates game the system?"

**Reality:**
- First interview exposes them to format/expectations (same for all candidates)
- Second interview can use different questions from same difficulty pool
- Gaming requires actual skill improvement (which is the goal)
- Data shows candidates improve, not "game" - they demonstrate true capability

## Conclusion

Karat's data demonstrates that equity and quality are not in tension - they reinforce each other. By removing bias and creating fair processes:

- **Companies find better candidates** (1,000+ hires they would have missed)
- **Candidates have better experiences** (second chances, transparency, fairness)
- **Diversity improves** (60% improvement rate for Black candidates, 41% close rate increase for women)
- **Quality remains high** (redo hires perform comparably to first-pass hires)

The key insight: **Traditional interviews often fail to measure true capability.** By giving candidates fair opportunities to demonstrate their skills, companies hire better while advancing equity.
