# Evidence-Based Structured Interviewing Research

This directory contains synthesized research findings on evidence-based structured interviewing practices from Google re:Work, Karat, academic meta-analyses, and organizational psychology research.

## Overview

Structured interviewing is the practice of asking all candidates for the same role identical questions and evaluating their responses using consistent, predetermined criteria. Research consistently shows this approach:

- **Doubles predictive validity** compared to unstructured interviews (0.51 vs 0.38)
- **Reduces bias** against protected groups by up to 50%
- **Increases candidate satisfaction** by 35% even among rejected candidates
- **Saves time** (~40 minutes per interview on average)

## Key Findings Summary

### What Works
- **Structured interviews** with predetermined questions and scoring rubrics
- **Behavioral questions** ("Tell me about a time when...") based on past performance
- **Situational questions** ("What would you do if...") for novel scenarios
- **Behaviorally anchored rating scales** (POOR/MIXED/GOOD/EXCELLENT) with observable indicators
- **Competency-based evaluation** tied directly to job requirements
- **Interviewer training and calibration** for consistency
- **Panel review** separating interviewers from final decision makers

### What Doesn't Work (or Introduces Bias)
- **Unstructured "conversational" interviews** with ad-hoc questions
- **Culture fit assessments** without clear, job-relevant definitions
- **Self-ratings** by candidates on any dimension
- **Brainteasers and puzzle questions** (no correlation with job performance)
- **Evaluating communication style, accent, or nervousness** when not job-relevant
- **Gut feelings** and post-hoc justifications
- **Educational pedigree** as primary signal (unless directly job-relevant)

## Validity Coefficients (Predictive Power)

From Schmidt & Hunter meta-analyses:

| Selection Method | Validity Coefficient |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Structured Interview** | **0.51** |
| Unstructured Interview | 0.38 |
| GMA (Cognitive Ability) + Structured Interview | 0.63 |
| Work Sample Tests | 0.54 |
| GMA (Cognitive Ability) | 0.51 |
| Integrity Tests | 0.41 |
| Personality Tests (Conscientiousness) | 0.31 |
| Years of Experience | 0.18 |
| Reference Checks | 0.26 |
| Age | -0.01 |

**Note:** Validity coefficients range from -1.0 to 1.0, where:
- 0.0 = no relationship to job performance
- 0.3 = weak relationship
- 0.5 = moderate relationship (considered very good for selection)
- 0.7+ = strong relationship (rare in personnel selection)

## Implementation Principles

### 1. Define Job-Relevant Competencies (3-5)
Extract critical competencies from job description. These must be:
- Observable and behavioral (not personality traits)
- Directly tied to job performance
- Measurable through interview questions

### 2. Develop Structured Questions
- Create 2-3 questions per competency
- Mix behavioral and situational questions
- Include predetermined follow-ups
- Avoid brainteasers and irrelevant puzzles

### 3. Create Behaviorally Anchored Rubrics
- Define 4 levels: POOR / MIXED / GOOD / EXCELLENT
- Use observable behaviors, not adjectives
- Include specific indicators for each level
- Document "non-signals" to ignore

### 4. Train and Calibrate Interviewers
- Explain the structure and rationale
- Practice scoring with example responses
- Calibrate as a group to ensure consistency
- Refresh training regularly

### 5. Separate Interviewing from Decision-Making
- Interviewers document evidence, not conclusions
- Hiring committee reviews all feedback independently
- Look for patterns across multiple interviewers
- Check for aberrations that may indicate bias

### 6. Measure and Iterate
- Track interview scores vs. actual job performance
- Monitor adverse impact across demographic groups
- Gather candidate feedback
- Update questions and rubrics based on data

## Equity and Fairness

Research shows structured interviews reduce adverse impact by **up to 50%** compared to unstructured approaches. Key equity practices:

### From Karat Research (2024 Data)
- **Redo interviews**: URM candidates 30% more likely to use; 60% of Black candidates improve scores
- **Resume-free screening**: Eliminates bias based on educational pedigree, names, etc.
- **Standardized questions**: Creates level playing field where all candidates assessed on same criteria
- **Close rate improvements**: 41% increase for female candidates with redo option
- **1,000+ hires** from redo interviews, 17% increase in overall hiring yield

### Non-Signals to Explicitly Ignore
Research shows these factors do NOT predict job performance but often bias evaluations:
- Communication style (introversion/extroversion when not job-relevant)
- Accent or non-native English speech patterns
- Nervousness, filler words, upspeak
- Educational pedigree (for experienced hires)
- Years of experience (weak predictor: r=0.18)
- Likability/"someone I'd have a beer with"
- Cultural similarity to interviewers
